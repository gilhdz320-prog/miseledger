# Photo shot list — for a Monday visit to Enderis 130

Eight photos, one phone, fifteen minutes. Shoot **horizontal** unless noted, and
**turn off HDR** so the stainless does not blow out.

The rule that matters most: **hands, not faces.** A hand weighing flour says more than
someone smiling at the camera. And shoot **tight** — a full room always looks smaller
than it is.

| File | What to shoot | Frame it like this |
|---|---|---|
| `hero.jpg` | The cook line with the hood and hanging pans, from the doorway | Vertical. Leave the left third empty: text sits there |
| `walk-1.jpg` | The entrance door with the light on, shot from inside the dark room | Vertical. Underexpose slightly; the light is the subject |
| `walk-2.jpg` | Shelves with boxes and jars, labels readable | Horizontal. Get low, shoot along the shelf |
| `walk-3.jpg` | Hands cutting something on a board, knife in frame | Horizontal. Top-down or 45°, wood board or white cutting board |
| `walk-4.jpg` | A pan over flame, or steam rising from a pot | Vertical. Focus on the flame/steam, not the pan handle |
| `pantry.jpg` | The scale with product on it, or a labeled container | Horizontal. Close enough that the number is readable |
| `list.jpg` | A clipboard or notebook with the week's list, on the counter | Horizontal. Slight angle, not perfectly flat |
| `notebook.jpg` | A tablet or phone showing the notebook, held in a hand | Horizontal. Screen legible, room soft behind |

## What to avoid

- Ceiling fluorescent as the only light: it turns everything green. Use the window light.
- Wide empty shots of the room. Tight and busy reads professional; wide and empty reads small.
- Anything with a visible person's face without their permission.
- Bins, mops, or clutter in frame — the camera finds them even when you do not.

Drop the files into this folder with the names above. Nothing else changes: the page
